What to Keep and What to Throw Away After Water Damage in Silver Spring

A practical sort, from clothing and furniture to food and building materials.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

After a leak or flood, the hardest questions are about stuff. Can the sofa be saved? What about the mattress, the clothes in the closet or the boxes in the basement? The answer depends on how clean the water was, how long things were wet and how porous they are.

Use this guide to sort items. When in doubt, photograph first and ask your insurer before you throw something out.

Start with the category of water

Clean water from a supply line is the most forgiving. Gray water from a washer or dishwasher is less so. Black water from sewage or floodwater is the most serious. Porous items exposed to black water are almost always discarded.

Items that can often be saved

  • Solid wood furniture, after cleaning and drying
  • Glass, ceramics and metal, after washing
  • Plastics that are not porous
  • Washable clothing exposed to clean water
  • Framed art, if it is handled by a conservator

Items that are usually discarded

  • Mattresses and upholstered furniture exposed to sewage or floodwater
  • Carpet pad and insulation
  • Particleboard furniture that swelled
  • Food and medicine that touched floodwater
  • Cardboard boxes and paper goods that stayed wet

Building materials

Drywall that has crumbled or stayed wet is usually removed. Solid wood framing can often be dried. Carpet exposed to clean water may be saved if it is lifted and dried fast, as covered on our carpet water damage restoration page.

Hardwood may cup or crack. See hardwood floor water damage repair for how boards are assessed.

Food and kitchen items

Discard food that touched floodwater or sewage, including items in cans, since the seal may be compromised. If the refrigerator lost power for a long period, follow food safety guidance. When in doubt, throw it out.

Clothing and textiles

Wash clothing exposed to clean water promptly, and dry it fully. Items exposed to sewage should be discarded or professionally treated. Do not let wet fabric sit in a pile, because growth can start in days.

Document before you discard

Photograph every item and write a short list. Ask your insurer whether they want to inspect anything. Some policies pay for contents, and a list helps. Keep a sample of a damaged material if the adjuster may want to see it.

How to dispose of heavy items safely

Wet carpet and furniture are heavy. Use help, wear gloves and keep a path clear. Check your township's rules for bulk trash pickup before putting anything at the curb.

Sentimental and valuable items

Some items matter far more than their price. Photographs, letters, heirloom furniture and family documents deserve a pause before you decide. Freeze paper items, set aside fabrics for professional cleaning and ask a specialist about valuable pieces.

Keep a short list of what you saved and what you discarded. It helps with your insurer and with your own memory later.

Children's items and bedding

Stuffed toys, pillows and mattresses are porous and hold water. After clean water, washable items can often be laundered on a hot cycle. After sewage or floodwater, replace them. Hard plastic toys can usually be washed with detergent and a disinfectant, then dried fully.

If you are unsure about a particular item, set it aside in a bag in a dry place and ask your restoration professional before you decide.

A walk-through order that saves time

  1. Make the area safe and stop the water.
  2. Photograph everything where it sits.
  3. Move dry, high-value items out of harm's way first.
  4. Pull out soaked porous items, such as rugs and boxes.
  5. Let drying begin before you sort the rest.

Sorting while the space is still wet slows drying. A good plan is to rescue what is time-sensitive, then let the crew start while you go through the rest with a clear head.

Pets, plants and living things

Keep pets out of wet rooms and away from cleaning products. Move houseplants to a dry, clean area. Pet bedding that touched floodwater should be replaced, and food bowls should be washed and disinfected.

When to ask a professional about an item

If an item is valuable, antique or tied to a claim, ask before you throw it away. A restoration professional can tell you whether it can be dried, cleaned or should go, and can explain the options in plain language.

A note for Silver Spring-area trash and cleanup

Disposal rules vary by municipality, and a large cleanup can produce a lot of debris. Ask your township or borough about bulk trash and dumpster rules before you start. Landisville is part of East Hempfield Township, while Columbia and Mount Joy are boroughs, so the office to ask differs.

If you are in Landisville or Mount Joy, ask your restoration professional to help plan removal so heavy items are not dragged through clean rooms.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

Can a wet mattress be saved?

After clean water, sometimes, if it is dried fast. After sewage or floodwater, no.

Should I keep wet books and photos?

Freeze them, if you can, to stop damage until they can be dried or treated. Do not leave them wet.

Do I have to keep everything for the adjuster?

Ask your insurer. Photographs and a list are often enough for low-value items.

If you are sorting after a leak, call the line first so drying can start while you sort.
