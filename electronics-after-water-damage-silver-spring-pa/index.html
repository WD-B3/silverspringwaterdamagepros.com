What to Do With Electronics After Water Damage in Silver Spring

How to handle wet devices, appliances and wiring without making things worse.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Water and electricity do not mix, and the first rule after a leak is to stay safe. Before you think about saving a laptop or a television, make sure no one is standing in water near a live outlet or cord.

This guide covers the order of operations: safety first, then power off, then drying and testing. It is general guidance and not a repair manual, and an electrician or a manufacturer should handle anything serious.

Safety before saving anything

If water is touching outlets, power strips or the electrical panel, do not walk into it. If you can reach the panel from a dry spot, turn off power to the affected area. If you cannot, leave the area and call for help. An electrician can check circuits before they are turned back on.

Power down and unplug

  • Do not turn on a device that has been wet
  • Unplug it if you can do so safely
  • Remove batteries if they come out easily
  • Do not charge a wet device

Phones and tablets

Power off, remove the case and the SIM tray, and dry the outside with a cloth. Leave the device in a dry place with air moving past it. The common advice to put it in rice is not reliable. Take it to a repair shop if it will not start after a day or two.

Laptops and computers

Power off, unplug, remove the battery if possible and turn the device upside down to drain. A repair shop can open it, clean it and test it. Ask about recovering data first. Back up whatever you can to another device before trying to turn it on.

Large appliances and televisions

A television or a refrigerator that was in standing water should not be plugged in until it is inspected. Water can damage controls and wiring in ways you cannot see. Ask a technician or the manufacturer about safe testing.

For kitchen and laundry losses, see appliance leak water damage and kitchen water damage restoration.

Wiring, outlets and the panel

Wiring that has been under water should be inspected by a licensed electrician before use. Outlets, switches and breakers can corrode. If you smell burning, see sparks or notice a tripping breaker, turn off the power at the main and leave the area.

HVAC and water heater controls

Furnaces, heat pumps and water heaters have controls that are sensitive to water. If any of them were wet, have a technician check them before use. See water heater leak cleanup for what happens when a tank fails.

Insurance and documentation

Photograph the devices, the model numbers and the damage. Keep any repair estimates. Some policies cover contents, with limits. Ask your insurer how they want electronics documented.

Data comes first

For many people the device matters less than what is on it. If a laptop or phone is wet, do not keep trying to turn it on. Each attempt can add damage. A repair shop can often recover data from a drive even when the device will not start.

Going forward, back up photos and files to storage that lives somewhere else. A wet basement can take a computer and its backup drive at the same time.

Small appliances and chargers

Chargers, power strips and extension cords that got wet should be thrown away. They are cheap to replace and unsafe to test. The same goes for small kitchen appliances that were under water, since you cannot see inside the housing.

Smart home devices and cameras

Hubs, cameras and thermostats are often installed low on walls or in basements where water reaches. If wet, unplug them and do not reconnect them until they have dried and been checked. Replacing a small device is often cheaper than risking a short.

A short order of operations

  1. Make the room safe and cut power if you can reach the panel dry.
  2. Unplug devices and remove batteries.
  3. Photograph model numbers and damage.
  4. Dry the room with professional help.
  5. Have an electrician check circuits before power is restored.

Replacing versus repairing

A repair makes sense for a costly device with valuable data or a short list of damaged parts. Replacement makes sense for older devices and anything that sat in dirty water. Ask the repair shop for a quote before you decide, and ask whether a diagnosis fee applies.

Contents coverage and electronics

Many policies cover contents with a limit and a deductible. Keep receipts, model numbers and photos to show what you owned. If you have high-value electronics, ask your agent whether the policy covers them or whether a separate rider is needed.

A note for Silver Spring-area storms

Storms often bring both water and power problems. In Lancaster County, a heavy rain can flood a basement while the power flickers. Landisville, in East Hempfield Township, drains toward Swarr Run, and low spots on a street can take water first.

If your basement in Landisville or Mountville has the panel, the furnace and the water heater down there, think about raising critical equipment or adding a sump pump with a backup. See basement water damage cleanup for the water side.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

Can I turn on a TV that got wet?

Not until it has dried and been checked. Turning it on can cause a short or permanent damage.

Is rice good for a wet phone?

It is not reliable. Power off, dry the outside and take it to a repair shop if it does not work.

When should I call an electrician?

Any time wiring, outlets or the panel were wet, or if you smell burning.
