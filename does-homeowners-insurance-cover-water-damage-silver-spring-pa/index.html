Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Silver Spring, PA?

A plain guide to how water damage claims are usually sorted, written for Lancaster County homeowners.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

The short answer is that it depends on where the water came from. Many homeowners policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or an appliance that fails. Many policies exclude water that rises from the ground, and many treat sewer backup as a separate add-on.

This guide explains the general pattern so you know which questions to ask. It is not a coverage opinion. Your policy and your insurer decide what applies, so read the policy language and call your agent early.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual

Most policies draw a line between water that arrives suddenly and water that arrives slowly. A supply line that splits overnight is usually treated as sudden. A drip under a sink that runs for months is usually treated as maintenance, and many policies exclude the damage it causes.

That line is why dates matter. When you file, you will be asked when you first noticed the water and what you did next. Write the timeline down while it is fresh, and keep photographs that show what the area looked like.

Flood and groundwater

Water that comes from outside the home, such as rain that rises over a yard, a creek that overflows or groundwater that pushes through a basement floor, is commonly excluded from standard homeowners policies. Flood coverage is usually a separate policy.

If your basement floods after a storm, ask your agent whether your policy has any water backup endorsement and whether you carry separate flood coverage. Our flood damage cleanup page explains what the restoration work involves either way.

Sewer and drain backup

A backup through a floor drain or toilet is often excluded unless you added an endorsement for it. If sewage is involved, the cleanup is different from clean water, and it should be handled with protective equipment. See sewage backup cleanup for the steps.

Mitigation is usually your job

Almost every policy expects you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. That means shutting off water, removing standing water and drying the structure. Emergency drying is often part of a claim, and the invoice and the readings that support it matter.

Keep receipts for anything you spend on temporary repairs, and ask your insurer what documentation they want before work begins.

Steps to take in the first day

  1. Stop the source if you can do so safely.
  2. Call the restoration line and your insurance company.
  3. Photograph and video every affected room and item.
  4. Write down when you first saw the water.
  5. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe.
  6. Ask for a written scope and price before work starts.

What a restoration company can and cannot do

A restoration company documents the loss, removes the water and dries the structure. It does not decide coverage, and it cannot promise what your insurer will pay. Be cautious of anyone who does.

You can ask the company for moisture readings, photographs and a written scope. These help the adjuster understand the loss, and they help you compare it with the estimate.

Deductibles and small losses

If the repair cost is close to your deductible, you may decide not to file. Claims can also affect future premiums. Talk with your agent about the tradeoffs before you decide, and ask whether reporting a loss and withdrawing it has any effect.

Reading the policy before you need it

The best time to read a policy is before a leak. Look for the section on perils, the list of exclusions and any endorsements. Search for the words sudden, accidental, seepage, continuous, flood, surface water, backup and mold. Each one changes what is covered.

Many people are surprised by limits and sublimits. A policy may cover water damage in general but cap certain categories, such as mold or sewer backup, at a lower amount. Ask your agent to explain any sublimit in plain terms.

Questions to ask your agent

  • Is a pipe that bursts inside a wall covered, and does the policy pay to access the pipe?
  • Does my policy include water backup coverage, and what is the limit?
  • Do I have flood coverage, and when does it take effect after purchase?
  • How does the policy treat a leak that went on for weeks?
  • What documentation do you need if I start emergency drying before an adjuster visits?

Roof leaks and wind-driven rain

Water entering through a roof is a common loss. Many policies cover it when the roof was damaged by a covered event, such as wind or a fallen branch, and exclude it when the cause is wear or lack of maintenance. Photographs of the opening and of the ceiling below help show which applies.

A leaking ceiling also raises drying questions. Our ceiling water damage repair page explains how the work is sequenced.

What not to do

  • Do not wait days to report a loss.
  • Do not sign a work order you have not read.
  • Do not throw away damaged items before documenting them.
  • Do not assume a contractor knows your coverage.

A note for Silver Spring and West Hempfield Township

Silver Spring is in West Hempfield Township, and the Susquehanna River forms a large share of the township's boundary. Homes closer to the river and its tributaries may face water from outside the house more often than homes on higher ground, which is why the flood question matters locally.

Mountville, a few miles south, drains toward Strickler Run and the West Branch of Little Conestoga Creek. If you live in Mountville or Columbia, check your policy and ask your agent whether a flood policy is in place.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many policies cover the damage from a sudden pipe failure, but not the cost of repairing the pipe itself. Your policy language decides. See burst pipe water damage for what the restoration work involves.

Is a basement flood from heavy rain covered?

Often it is not, unless you have flood coverage or a water backup endorsement. Ask your agent which applies.

Does insurance cover drying after a storm in West Hempfield Township?

It depends on how the water got in. Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof is treated differently from rising water. Ask your insurer which category your loss falls under.

If water is spreading now, stop reading and call. Documentation matters later, but the first hour matters most.
