Water Damage Restoration in Ironville, Silver Spring, PA

Ironville is a small West Hempfield Township community less than two miles from Silver Spring. If water is spreading in a home there, call the line.

17512

Ironville is an unincorporated community in West Hempfield Township, Lancaster County. An 1875 atlas of the county describes it as a village on Chestnut Hill, laid out by John K. Smith, in the part of the township known for iron ore. Today its best-known landmark is Ironville Community Park on Prospect Road.

Many Ironville addresses use a Columbia mailing address with ZIP code 17512, but the ZIP code is only a mail label. Ironville is part of West Hempfield Township, not Columbia borough, and this page is about the community, not the borough.

Where Ironville sits

Map sources place Ironville less than two miles from Silver Spring, and about two miles from both Mountville and Columbia. Prospect Road, Fairview Road and Ironville Pike are the streets people use to describe where they are. The community has no official boundary that we can point to, so on a call it helps to give the nearest cross street.

If you are closer to a borough than to the community center, our pages for Mountville and Columbia describe how we reach those places.

A community that has its own emergency history

The marker at Ironville Community Park explains that the Ironville Fire Company was incorporated in 1944 and merged with the Silver Spring Fire Company in 2000. It was an all-volunteer company that served West Hempfield Township and raised money with summer festivals.

That history has a practical lesson. A fire company, a utility or 911 handles immediate dangers such as fire, gas or a person in danger. Our line handles the water damage that follows. If you smell gas, see sparks or fear a collapse, call 911 or the utility first.

When water is spreading in Ironville

The line is open around the clock for water damage calls from Ironville. A restoration professional asks where the water is coming from, how far it has spread and whether power is nearby, then helps you plan the first steps.

Any home can have a supply line fail, a water heater leak or a washing machine hose split. Those are the usual causes of a call. We do not claim that one kind of damage is more common in Ironville than elsewhere, because we have no local records that say so.

  • Standing water in a basement or on a first floor
  • A burst or frozen pipe inside a wall or ceiling
  • A water heater, dishwasher or washer that has leaked into flooring
  • Rain or storm water entering through a foundation wall or window well

What the work looks like

A typical visit starts with finding the source and measuring what is wet. Standing water is removed first with pumps or extractors, as described on our water extraction page. Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry walls, floors and framing, and moisture readings show when the structure is back to normal. See structural drying for how that is tracked.

Lower levels get special attention. Our basement water damage cleanup page explains pumping, drying and what usually has to be removed. For a pipe failure, see burst pipe water damage.

Rain, runoff and what we can say about flood zones

West Hempfield Township operates a stormwater program, and the township website lists stormwater and MS4 information. That tells you the township manages runoff. It does not tell you that any particular street in Ironville floods.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. If you want to know about your own address, use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, which looks up property addresses. A mapped zone does not predict damage in either direction, and water can enter a home from plumbing, appliances or a roof with no flooding at all.

Documenting damage for your insurer

Photographs of visible damage, moisture readings and a written scope can help describe what happened and what was done. Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the terms of your policy, so read your policy and ask your insurer what it covers. We cannot tell you what an insurer will pay.

Before We Arrive: steps for an Ironville home

Do only what is safe. Skip any step that puts you near electrical hazards or contaminated water, and say so on the call.

Stop the source

Close the valve for the failed fixture or the main valve if you can reach it safely.

Stay away from electrics

Keep out of rooms where water touches outlets, cords or the panel. Do not switch breakers while standing in water.

Protect what is dry

Lift small items, rugs and boxes off wet floors if it is safe to do so.

Take photos

Photograph the damage and the source before anything is moved.

Ironville water damage questions

Is emergency water removal available in Ironville?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes emergency calls from Ironville at any hour, and a restoration professional will say what is possible that day once they have the address.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Ironville?

Many Ironville addresses use ZIP code 17512, which is a Columbia mailing code. A ZIP code is not a boundary, so call with your street address and we will confirm whether we can reach it.

Is Ironville part of Columbia?

Not as a municipality. Ironville is in West Hempfield Township, even though its mail often carries a Columbia address.

Is my Ironville home in a flood zone?

We have not verified flood-zone status for the community as a whole. Check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Water damage can happen outside mapped zones.

What if the water came from a sewer or drain?

Say so on the call. Sewage needs protective gear and a different plan, and our sewage backup cleanup page explains the steps.
