Documenting Water Damage for an Insurance Claim in Silver Spring, PA

What to photograph, what to write down and what to keep, so your claim is easier to explain.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

A claim is a story told with evidence. The adjuster was not in your house when the water arrived, so the photos, notes and records you collect are how they understand what happened and how bad it was.

Good documentation does not decide whether the claim is approved. It does make the conversation faster and the numbers easier to check. This guide lists what to gather, in the order that works best when you are stressed and short on time.

Photos and video first

Start with wide shots of each room so the layout is clear. Then move closer to wet walls, ceilings, floors and furniture. Take a short video as you walk through, and say the room name aloud. Capture the source if you can see it, such as a split pipe or a failed hose.

Take photos before anything is moved or thrown away. If you must remove something to prevent harm, photograph it first and keep a sample of damaged material if the adjuster may want to see it.

Write the timeline

Note when you first noticed the water, when you shut it off, who you called and when. Insurers often ask for this, and memory fades fast. A few lines in a phone note are enough.

Keep a list of damaged items

List each damaged item with a rough age, the room and an estimate of value. Photos of serial numbers, labels and receipts help. For items that cannot be saved, our guide to deciding what to keep and what to discard will help you sort them, but do not throw anything away before your insurer says it is fine.

Ask for moisture readings

A restoration professional uses a moisture meter to find water in walls and floors you cannot see. Ask for a copy of the readings, the date and the locations. They show where water went and, later, that the structure dried.

Our structural drying page describes how daily readings are logged until the building reaches normal levels.

Save every receipt

  • Plumber or emergency repair invoices
  • Equipment or supplies you bought for cleanup
  • Hotel or meal costs if you cannot stay home, if your policy may cover them
  • The restoration company's written scope and invoice

Get the scope in writing

Ask for a written scope of work before any project begins. It should say what will be removed, what will be dried and what will be rebuilt, and how the price is built up. Our guide to reading a scope covers this in more detail.

A written scope also helps you compare the restoration estimate with the adjuster's estimate, which can differ.

Keep communication in one place

Save emails and texts, and note the date and name of any phone call with the insurer. If an adjuster visits, ask for their contact information and a summary of what they saw.

A sample room-by-room record

A simple table in a notes app keeps the claim organized. For each room, record the date, the visible damage, the materials involved and the photos you took. Keep file names consistent so you can find them later, for example kitchen-wall-1 or basement-floor-3.

If you are using a phone, turn on location and time stamps for photos. They are not proof, but they help show when each picture was taken.

Photographing damage that is hard to see

Some damage does not show well in a photo. Staining may be faint, and swelling may only be visible from the side. Use a flashlight held at a low angle to show warped boards and raised seams. Place a coin or a ruler beside the damage to show scale.

For ceiling stains, take one wide shot of the room and one close shot of the stain. Mark the edge of the stain with a pencil and date it, so changes over the next days are clear.

Before-and-after records

If you have photos from before the loss, such as a holiday, a renovation or a real estate listing, save them. They show the prior condition of floors, walls and finishes. They are particularly helpful for hardwood, custom cabinets and finished basements.

After drying and repair, photograph the same areas again from the same angle. A matched set of before, during and after photos is easy for anyone to follow.

Working with the adjuster

Be present for the inspection if you can. Walk the adjuster through each room in the order you documented them. Share your photos, your timeline and the restoration company's scope and readings. Ask when you should expect a written estimate and who to contact with questions.

If the adjuster's number differs from the scope you received, ask for a line-by-line comparison. Sometimes the difference is a different assumption about what can be dried and what must be replaced.

A note for Silver Spring and nearby towns

In West Hempfield Township and the surrounding boroughs, many homes have basements. Basements are where most documentation gets lost, because water is often gone by the time the adjuster arrives. Take photos of high-water marks on walls and the contents of the lowest level as soon as you can.

If you live in Landisville or Mount Joy, ask your restoration professional to mark the date and location on each moisture reading. See basement water damage cleanup for how basement losses are measured.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

How many photos should I take?

More than you think. Several wide shots per room, close shots of each damaged surface and any visible source are a good start.

Should I wait for the adjuster before cleaning up?

Not if water is still spreading. Stop the damage first, document it and tell your insurer what you did. Ask whether they want to see anything before it is removed.

Can a restoration company file my claim for me?

Usually the policyholder files. A company can provide documentation, readings and a scope, but it should not speak for you on coverage.

If you have a wet house today, call first, then document. Help can start while you take photos.
