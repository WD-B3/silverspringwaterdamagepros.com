Water Damage Restoration in Chestnut Hill, Silver Spring, PA

Chestnut Hill is an iron-ore hill and community in West Hempfield Township near Silver Spring. If water is spreading in a home there, call the line.

Chestnut Hill is a place name that covers both a hill and the community on it, in West Hempfield Township, Lancaster County. The 1875 county atlas says hematite iron ore was found there and mined for decades, and that the villages of Ironville and Kinderhook sit on or near the hill.

Most people today know the area by its parks. Lake Grubb Nature Park and Grubb Lake, which the Silver Spring reference describes as part of the former Chestnut Hill Iron Ore Mine, are useful landmarks when you need to tell someone where you are.

Finding your location on the hill

Chestnut Hill sits a few miles northeast of Columbia and close to Silver Spring. Map sources give its elevation as about 520 feet, which is an estimate for the surrounding area. We do not claim that elevation makes homes safer or more exposed to water, because it does not decide how water gets into a house.

On a call, give the road name and the nearest cross street. Landmarks such as Lake Grubb Nature Park and Silver Spring Park help when a street name is unclear.

Ironville and Kinderhook are neighbors

Kinderhook and Ironville are small neighboring communities on the same hill. Our page for Ironville covers that side of the hill. Kinderhook is mentioned here only as a neighbor, because we did not find enough independent information to write a separate page about it.

When water is spreading in Chestnut Hill

The line is open around the clock for Chestnut Hill. A restoration professional asks about the source, the size of the wet area and any electrical hazards, then helps you plan the first steps while a crew is arranged.

In a residential area like this one, most calls start inside the house. A supply line fails, an appliance leaks or a water heater gives out. See appliance leak water damage and water heater leak cleanup for how those losses spread and how they are cleaned up.

Water from above: roofs and ceilings

Water that comes in through a roof or an upstairs fixture can travel through insulation and ceilings before it shows. A brown stain or a sagging ceiling means water is already there. Keep out from under a sagging ceiling, and call. Our ceiling water damage repair page describes how the work is sequenced.

Drying after the water is out

Removing water is only the start. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings from a moisture meter confirm it. If drying stops early, hidden moisture can lead to damage and growth, which is why our structural drying page puts readings first.

If you find a musty smell or dark growth later, see mold remediation. We make no health claims about mold.

Rain, runoff and what is not verified

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for Chestnut Hill. We also found no verified record of a named storm or flood that damaged homes on the hill, so we do not describe it as flood-prone.

Heavy rain can still send water into any basement. If that happens, see flood damage cleanup, and use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to look up your own address.

Insurance documentation

Photos, moisture readings and a written scope can help describe the damage and the work. What your policy covers depends on the cause and its terms, so ask your insurer. We cannot promise coverage or an outcome.

Before We Arrive: steps for a Chestnut Hill home

Stay safe first. If any step puts you near electrical hazards or dirty water, skip it and tell us on the call.

Shut off the source

Close the valve for the failed fixture or the main valve if it is safe to reach.

Avoid wet electrics

Do not touch outlets or the panel in wet areas. Do not run household fans in standing water.

Keep clear of ceilings

Stay out from under a sagging or bulging ceiling.

Photograph first

Take photos of the source and the damage before cleanup starts.

Do I need to call for a small leak?
