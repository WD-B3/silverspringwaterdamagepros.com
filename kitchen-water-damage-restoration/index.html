Kitchen Water Damage in Silver Spring, PA

Cabinets, countertops and flooring hold water in places you cannot see, so kitchens need a careful moisture check.

Kitchen water damage in a Silver Spring home often starts under the sink or behind an appliance, and it shows up as swollen cabinet bases, warped flooring or a musty smell. Close the shutoff valve and call the 24/7 line for kitchen water damage restoration before the damage spreads.

A kitchen is full of water sources, from the sink supply lines and dishwasher to the refrigerator ice maker, and it is full of materials that react badly to moisture, including particleboard cabinets, laminate counters and vinyl or wood flooring.

Water under the kitchen sink

Under-sink leaks are the most common kitchen water losses, and they can go unnoticed for weeks because the cabinet hides them.

  • Look for dark stains or swelling on the cabinet floor.
  • Check supply line connections and the drain trap for drips.
  • Feel the back of the cabinet and the wall for dampness.
  • Smell for mustiness when you open the doors.

How kitchen water damage is handled

The work focuses on opening what hides moisture.

  1. Stop the source and remove standing water, see water extraction.
  2. Remove toe kicks and cabinet bases when readings show water behind them.
  3. Check subfloor, wall cavities and flooring with a moisture meter and thermal camera.
  4. Dry the kitchen with air movers and drying panels until readings are normal.
  5. Rebuild, which may include cabinet, flooring and drywall repair.

Appliances versus plumbing

A dishwasher, refrigerator or sink line can each be the source, and the page for appliance leak water damage explains the appliance side in more detail. This page focuses on the cabinets, counters and flooring the water reaches.

A plumber or appliance technician fixes the failed part. Restoration then dries and repairs the kitchen. If the leak started upstairs, the ceiling below may need work as well, see ceiling water damage.

Countertops, backsplashes and what stays

Laminate countertops swell at the edges when water gets under them, and particleboard cores do not recover. Stone and solid-surface tops usually survive, but the cabinet bases beneath them may not. A moisture meter tells you what is wet under a surface that looks fine.

Tile backsplashes usually stay if the wall behind them dried in time. If the drywall behind the tile stayed wet, grout can fail and mold can grow where you cannot see it.

Dishwasher, refrigerator and sink lines in the kitchen

Three lines cause most kitchen leaks. The sink supply connections can drip behind the cabinet doors, a dishwasher inlet or drain hose can leak onto the floor each cycle, and a refrigerator ice maker line can drip behind the unit for weeks. A flashlight and a dry paper towel run along each connection often finds a slow leak.

If you find a leak, shut the supply valve and call a plumber or appliance technician. Restoration then dries what the water reached.

Kitchen drying timeline

Kitchens dry in stages. Water comes out first, cabinet bases and flooring are opened, and equipment runs while readings are logged. Tile and stone floors usually dry quickly, while wood and laminate need longer and sometimes replacement.

Plan on several days of equipment, during which cooking may be limited. A restoration professional can tell you which appliances to leave in place and which to move.

Cost factors and insurance

Cabinet and flooring replacement usually drives kitchen costs. Sudden leaks are often covered under a homeowners policy, while slow seepage often is not. Ask your insurer and photograph the kitchen before cleanup.

Kitchen water damage questions from Silver Spring homeowners

Can swollen cabinets be saved?

Solid wood can often be dried and refinished, while particleboard that has swelled is usually replaced.

Is a small under-sink leak worth calling about?

Yes, if the cabinet base is soft or the wall is damp. Small leaks hide larger damage.

Will the kitchen floor need replacing?

It depends on the material and how long it was wet. A moisture meter and a visual check give a clear answer.

Do I need to empty the cabinets?

Usually yes, for access and protection. Items that were wet may need cleaning or disposal.
