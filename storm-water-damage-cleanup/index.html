Emergency Storm Water Damage in Silver Spring, PA

Storms drive water through roofs, windows and siding. Cleanup starts with protecting the building and removing wet materials.

Storm water damage in a Silver Spring home usually means rain got through a damaged roof, window or wall, and the 24/7 line is open once it is safe to call. Stay away from downed lines and unstable structures, then call for emergency storm water damage cleanup to dry the building and limit the loss.

This page covers wind and rain damage through the building envelope. Rising water from creeks or the river is a flood, covered on the flood damage page, and a plain roof leak is handled with the ceiling page.

When storm water is an emergency

Storms create hazards beyond the water.

  • A tree has hit the roof or a window has been blown out.
  • Rain is entering through a damaged wall or roof opening.
  • Power lines are down or the service entrance is damaged.
  • Ceilings are sagging under rainwater.

Stay out of any area with downed lines. A temporary tarp or board-up can limit rain while repairs are arranged.

Storm cleanup and drying

Wind damage usually needs a roofer and a restoration team.

  1. Make the building safe and tarp or board openings.
  2. Remove standing water and soaked contents, see water extraction.
  3. Remove wet insulation and any drywall that has absorbed water.
  4. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, see structural drying.
  5. Document damage with photos for the insurer.

Storms and the Susquehanna

Two storms are useful reference points. At the National Weather Service gauge in Marietta, the river crested at 56.28 feet on September 20, 2004 during Hurricane Ivan, and at 58.16 feet on September 9, 2011 during Tropical Storm Lee. Both events combined heavy rain and river rise.

For a home near the river, a storm can mean wind-driven rain on one side and rising water on the other. Keep the two separate in your notes, because insurers treat wind and flood very differently.

Insurance for storm damage

Wind and hail damage is usually covered under a standard homeowners policy, while flood is not. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises asking whether wind and flood are covered, photographing the damage and making only the repairs needed to prevent more damage until your insurer approves permanent ones.

Documenting storm damage for a claim

Take wide photos of each affected room and close photos of every damaged item. Photograph the opening where water came in, such as the roof damage or the broken window. Note the date and time of the storm and when you first noticed the water. This record helps the insurer understand the loss and supports the written scope.

Storm Water Damage questions from Silver Spring homeowners

Do I need a roofer or a restoration company?

Usually both. The roofer repairs the opening and the restoration team handles wet materials.

Is storm water damage covered by insurance?

Wind and hail are usually covered, while rising flood water is not. Ask your insurer which applies.

How long can wet insulation stay?

Not long. Wet insulation holds moisture against framing and drywall and is usually removed.

Can I tarp the roof myself?

Only if it is safe to do so. Working on a wet roof is dangerous, and a professional can handle it.

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Silver Spring?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, including during storms. Same-day response is offered when a professional is available, once it is safe to reach the property.
