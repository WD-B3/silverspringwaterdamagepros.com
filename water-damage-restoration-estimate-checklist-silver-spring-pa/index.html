A Water Damage Restoration Estimate Checklist for Silver Spring Homeowners

What a clear estimate includes, what changes the price and what to ask before you approve it.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

A water damage estimate is easier to judge when you know what should be in it. Some estimates are a single number. Others list every step. The more detail, the easier it is to compare and to explain to your insurer.

Use this checklist when you read an estimate. It does not give prices, because costs depend on the size and type of the loss, but it shows what a careful estimate looks like.

The basics

  • The company name, address and contact details
  • The property address and date of loss
  • A description of the source and type of water
  • The rooms and square footage affected
  • The date of the estimate and how long it is valid

Water removal

A line for extraction should say what area and what method. Standing water may need pumps or extractors. If carpet is being lifted, the estimate should say so. See water extraction for how this works.

Drying equipment and monitoring

A good estimate lists the equipment, such as air movers and dehumidifiers, the number of units and the days. It also lists monitoring visits and moisture readings. A bare line that says drying with no detail is hard to check.

Ask who removes the equipment and when. See structural drying for what daily monitoring involves.

Demolition and disposal

Removal of wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad is a separate line. It should say what is being removed, in what areas and to what height. Disposal fees should be shown.

Cleaning and treatment

Cleaning surfaces, treating odors and handling contaminated materials may be included. If sewage is involved, ask how it is handled. See sewage backup cleanup.

Repairs and rebuild

Some estimates stop at mitigation. Others add repairs. If repairs are included, the estimate should list materials and finishes, such as drywall, paint and flooring, and should state what is not included.

What changes the price

  • The size of the affected area
  • The type of water and its category
  • The materials involved, such as hardwood or plaster
  • The time the water has been present
  • Access, such as crawl spaces and second floors
  • Whether work is needed outside normal hours

Terms to read

Check how changes are handled. A fair estimate says what happens if more damage is found. Check payment terms and whether you are signing a direction to pay your insurer. Read before you sign.

Compare with care

Line up two estimates side by side. Do both include equipment, monitoring, removal and disposal? A lower total may leave things out. Ask each company to explain any difference.

Photos and readings that belong with the estimate

An estimate is easier to trust when it comes with evidence. Ask for photos of the affected areas and a copy of the moisture readings that support the scope. These help your insurer, and they help you check that the estimate matches the loss.

If the company cannot show readings, ask why. Being unable to measure is a red flag for the whole job.

A simple checklist to carry

  1. Is the scope in writing?
  2. Does it list water removal, drying, monitoring and removal separately?
  3. Does it show quantities and units?
  4. Does it explain what happens if more damage is found?
  5. Does it say who removes the equipment and when?
  6. Does it separate mitigation from repair?

Reading the total

A total tells you very little on its own. Ask for the subtotals: removal, equipment, monitoring, demolition, cleaning and repairs. Subtotals let you see where the money goes and which parts are fixed and which depend on what is found.

If an estimate gives an allowance for something unknown, such as hidden damage behind a wall, ask how the allowance is reconciled at the end.

Estimates for commercial losses

A shop or office loss has extra pieces, such as working around business hours and protecting inventory. Our commercial water damage restoration page explains how those jobs are planned. Ask for those items to be listed so they can be compared.

When an estimate is only a starting point

Some jobs cannot be priced exactly until walls are opened. In that case, ask for a range and a clear trigger for changes. A fair arrangement is that the company stops and calls you when something new is found, explains it and waits for your approval before adding cost.

Keep your own notes of each call. If a number changes later, you will want the date of the conversation and the reason given.

Timing of payment and insurance

Ask when the first payment is due and whether it depends on an insurance check. Insurers often pay in stages, and you may be responsible for the deductible up front. Knowing this early helps you plan.

A note for Silver Spring-area losses

Basements are common in West Hempfield Township, and basement jobs often have more access issues, such as stairs and narrow doors, which can change labor. In Mount Joy, tight lots and older streets can affect where equipment is staged.

If you are comparing quotes for a job in Mount Joy or Landisville, ask each company how it plans access and whether travel is part of the price.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

Should the estimate be in writing?

Yes. A written estimate lets you compare and gives your insurer something to review.

Why do two estimates differ so much?

They may cover different scope, such as readings, equipment days or rebuilding. Ask each to explain.

Can the price change after work starts?

It can if more damage is found. A good estimate says how changes are handled.
