Questions to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company in Silver Spring, PA

What to ask before you sign, and what a good answer sounds like.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

When water is spreading, there is pressure to say yes to the first company that answers. A few minutes of questions can save a lot of trouble. The goal is not to be difficult. The goal is to learn how the job will run before you commit.

Below are the questions that matter most, with notes on what a good answer looks like. Keep this list handy so you can read it by phone.

Who will actually do the work?

Ask whether the people on site are employees or subcontractors, and who will be in charge on the first day. A clear answer is a good sign. Vague answers are not.

Are you licensed and insured?

Ask what licenses and insurance apply to the work, and ask for proof of insurance. Rules vary by state and by type of work, so ask the company to explain what applies. Do not accept a general claim without a document.

Will you give me a written scope and price before work starts?

This is the most important question. A written scope lists what will be removed, dried and repaired. A price explained before work starts means no surprises later. Be careful of anyone who says they cannot tell you anything until the work is done.

How will you measure that the structure is dry?

A good answer includes moisture meters, readings at set intervals and a dry reference. Our structural drying page describes this. If the answer is only that equipment will run for three days, ask what happens if the readings are not normal on day three.

What equipment will you use, and for how long?

Ask how many air movers and dehumidifiers, where they will go and what the electricity use will be. Ask who removes the equipment and when.

How do you handle contaminated water?

If sewage or floodwater is involved, ask about protective gear, containment and disposal. See sewage backup cleanup for what a careful job looks like.

Will you work with my insurance company?

A company can provide documentation and a scope. It should not tell you what the insurer will pay or promise a result. If the company asks for a direction to pay, read it before you sign.

Red flags

  • Pressure to sign immediately, before you see a scope
  • No written price or a price that changes with no explanation
  • Promises about insurance outcomes
  • No moisture meter on site
  • Reluctance to share photos or readings

After the job

Ask for a final report that includes the readings, photos and a list of what was removed. Keep it with your insurance records, because a buyer or lender may ask for it later.

Questions about timing and communication

Ask when someone can be on site and how you will be updated. Timing is hard to promise in an emergency, so be wary of anyone who guarantees a specific minute. A better answer is a plan and a phone number you can call if things change.

Ask who your contact will be for the length of the job, and how to reach them in the evening. Clear communication matters more than a fast first call.

Questions about your belongings

Ask how contents will be handled. Will items be moved, stored or cleaned? Who is responsible if something is damaged during the work? Ask for a list of items moved and where they are kept.

If you have valuable or fragile items, tell the crew before work begins, and photograph them first.

Questions about payment

Ask what is due before work starts, what is due when it finishes and what happens if your insurer pays late. Ask whether you will be asked to sign a direction to pay and what it means. A fair company explains these terms in plain language.

Questions about reviews and references

Ask for references from recent jobs similar to yours. Check how the company handled problems, not only how happy people were when things went well. Be careful with review pages, since scores alone do not show the quality of the work. A company that can show past scopes and readings can show its work.

Questions about what happens next

Before you hang up or sign, ask what the next three steps are, who does them and when you will hear from the company again. This gives you a plan and gives the company a commitment to explain. Write the answers down.

Trust your sense of the call

A company that listens, asks about the source and the safety of the space, and explains without jargon is usually a good sign. A company that rushes you or avoids questions is a signal to slow down.

A note for Silver Spring and the towns around it

Local knowledge matters in a county where the housing stock ranges from older rowhouses to newer builds. Ask whether the company has worked in your town and knows its basements, its drainage and its water service. In Mount Joy, for instance, water and sewer run through the Mount Joy Borough Authority, so a leak at the street is a call to the authority.

Companies that serve Mount Joy and Columbia should be able to explain how local conditions change the plan.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

Should I get more than one estimate?

If water is still spreading, stop the damage first. Then, if time allows, compare scopes from more than one company.

Is the cheapest estimate the best?

Not necessarily. Compare what is included, such as readings, equipment and removal, instead of only the total.

Do I need to be home during the work?

Usually you need to be there at the start and for key decisions. Ask the company what they need from you.
