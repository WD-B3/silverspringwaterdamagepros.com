How to Prevent Water Damage While Away From Your Silver Spring Home

A checklist for trips, winters away and any time the house will sit empty.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Most big water losses are made worse by time. A pipe that splits while you are at work gets found in hours. The same pipe splitting during a two-week trip can run for days. Preparing the house before you leave is the cheapest protection you have.

This checklist covers the main risks in order of how often they cause trouble: supply lines, appliances, heat and drainage. None of it guarantees anything, but all of it lowers the odds.

Know where your main shutoff is

Find the main water valve and make sure everyone in the household can turn it. It may be near the water heater, in a basement corner or where the line enters the house. Turn it a quarter turn to check that it moves. A valve that has not moved in years can stick.

For trips longer than a few days, many people shut the main off. If you have a pipe failure while it is off, no water runs.

Check hoses and supply lines

Washing machine hoses, toilet supply lines and dishwasher connections are common failure points. Look for bulges, cracks, rust or drips. Braided stainless steel hoses last longer than rubber ones. Replace anything that looks tired before you leave.

Our appliance leak water damage page explains how these losses spread and what cleanup involves.

Water heater and basement

Look at the base of the tank and the floor around it. A rust trail or a puddle is a warning. If the tank is older than ten years, think about having it inspected. See water heater leak cleanup for what happens when one fails.

Heat in winter

  • Keep the thermostat set to a temperature that keeps pipes from freezing, and do not turn heat off completely
  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls so warm air reaches pipes
  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and close the valves that feed them
  • Ask a neighbor or friend to check the house if a cold snap is forecast

Sump pump and gutters

Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit and watching it start. Check that the discharge pipe is clear and points away from the house. Clear the gutters so rain moves away from the foundation instead of down the walls.

A backup battery for the pump is worth considering if storms often knock the power out where you live.

Smart sensors and shutoff valves

Water sensors placed near the water heater, washer, sinks and sump pit can send an alert to your phone. Some systems shut the water off automatically. They help only if you or someone you trust sees the alert, so choose a setup that fits your trip.

Who should check the house

Ask someone to walk through every few days. Give them the shutoff location, your phone number and a plan for what to do if they find water. Tell them not to touch electrical equipment if floors are wet.

If they find a problem, they should call the 24/7 line and then call you. Describing the source and the rooms affected helps the crew plan.

A one-page leaving checklist

  1. Shut off the main water or the washer valves
  2. Set the heat to a safe level
  3. Check hoses and the water heater
  4. Test the sump pump
  5. Leave your contact information and the shutoff location with a neighbor

Garage, laundry and second floors

Second-floor laundry rooms and bathrooms cause some of the most expensive leaks, because water travels down through ceilings and walls to the rooms below. If your washer is upstairs, think about a drain pan with a drain line and turn off the washer valves whenever you leave for a few days.

Garages with a water heater or a utility sink deserve a look too. A small drip can run for a long time with no one around to see it.

Ice, thaw and roofs

Winter brings roof leaks from ice buildup and thaw. A quick look at the ceiling in the rooms below the roof before you leave can catch a stain early. If you see one, photograph it and call a roofer, since a small stain can grow quickly.

After you come back

Walk the house when you return. Look at ceilings, under sinks, around the water heater and in the basement. Run a faucet and flush a toilet to check for drips. A quick walk can catch a slow leak before it grows.

A note for Silver Spring winters and storms

Lancaster County winters bring freezing nights, and older homes in towns like Mountville, laid out in 1814 and incorporated as a borough in 1906, often have plumbing in exterior walls or unheated crawl spaces. Those are the places pipes freeze first.

If you spend winters away, tell a neighbor in Mountville or Landisville how to reach us. See frozen pipe water damage for what thawing damage looks like.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

Should I turn off the water when I go on vacation?

For trips of several days it is a common precaution. Check your water heater manual first, since some tanks should be left on or set to a vacation mode.

Is it safe to leave the heat off in winter?

Not in a cold climate. Pipes can freeze when the house gets cold, so keep the heat at a safe setting.

What if I come home to a wet floor?

Shut off the water if you can do so safely, stay clear of electrical equipment and call the 24/7 line.

If you come home to water, do not wait. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.
