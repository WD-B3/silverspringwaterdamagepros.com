Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Mold in Silver Spring, PA?

How mold is usually treated in a homeowners policy and the questions that clarify your own coverage.

By the Silver Spring Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Mold coverage is one of the most confusing parts of a homeowners policy. Some policies cover it when it follows a covered water loss. Some cap it at a low limit. Some exclude it entirely. The only answer that counts is the one in your own policy.

This guide explains the common patterns so you know what to ask. It is general information, not a coverage opinion, and it does not make health claims about mold. If you have questions about your policy, call your agent or your insurer.

Cause matters more than the mold

Insurers usually look at why the mold appeared. If a pipe burst, the water damage was covered and mold followed quickly, the insurer may treat the growth as part of that covered loss. If mold grew from a long-term leak, high humidity or a house that was never dried, the insurer may call it a maintenance issue.

That is why the timeline you keep matters. Note when the water arrived, when you reported it and when drying began.

Limits and sublimits

Even when mold is covered, the policy may set a separate limit. A limit that is much lower than the dwelling coverage is common. Ask what the limit is, whether it applies to testing and cleanup, and whether it applies per claim or per year.

Ask also whether the limit covers the cost of tearing out and replacing materials, or only the cleaning.

Common exclusions

  • Mold from long-term leaks or seepage
  • Mold caused by high indoor humidity or poor ventilation
  • Mold from flooding, unless separate flood coverage applies
  • Mold in a house that was left unmaintained
  • Mold that appears after the insured failed to mitigate a loss

Mitigation duty

Almost every policy expects you to protect the property from further damage once a loss happens. That means drying promptly. If you leave a wet house for weeks and growth follows, the insurer may argue that you did not meet that duty.

Fast action also helps in practice. Our structural drying page explains how quick drying reduces what has to be removed later.

Testing and who pays for it

Some insurers require testing, and some will not pay for it. Ask before ordering. If you order testing yourself, keep the report with your claim records. If a buyer or lender wants proof of cleanup later, ask what documents they accept.

How to start a mold claim

  1. Report the water loss as soon as you can and keep the date.
  2. Photograph the growth and the likely moisture source.
  3. Ask your agent how mold is treated in your policy.
  4. Get a written scope from a restoration professional before removing materials.
  5. Keep the scope, readings, invoices and any clearance report together.

What a restoration company can tell you

A company can describe what it sees, record moisture readings and write a scope. It cannot tell you whether the insurer will pay. Our mold remediation page describes how the scope is built so you can compare it with the adjuster's estimate.

If a basement is involved, see basement water damage cleanup for how water and growth are handled together.

If the claim is denied

Ask for the denial in writing and for the policy language it relies on. You can ask for a review, and you can ask your state insurance department how to file a complaint. Keep your records, because they matter in any appeal.

Selling a home after a mold issue

Buyers and lenders may ask about past moisture problems. Keep the scope, readings and invoices from the cleanup, and ask your agent or attorney what you must disclose. Documentation that shows the cause was fixed and the structure was dried helps the conversation.

Questions to ask your agent about mold

  • Is mold covered when it follows a covered water loss?
  • What is the mold limit, and does it include testing and removal?
  • Does the limit apply per claim or per year?
  • Does the policy exclude mold from long-term seepage?
  • Is there an endorsement that raises the limit?

Keeping records that help

Mold claims are decided largely on records. Keep the date you first noticed water, photographs of the growth, moisture readings and the written scope. A single folder, paper or digital, with everything in order saves hours later.

Safe steps while you wait on a claim

  • Stop the water source and keep the area closed off
  • Do not scrub, sand or vacuum visible growth
  • Photograph the growth and the moisture source before anything is removed
  • Keep receipts for temporary repairs and drying
  • Talk with a physician about any health questions

A note for Silver Spring and Mount Joy

West Hempfield Township has a large amount of water in it, since the Susquehanna River makes up most of the township's 2.1 square miles of water, and humid river summers are a fact of life in Lancaster County. That makes moisture control a regular concern for local homeowners.

In Mount Joy, Little Chiques Creek crosses the east side of the borough, and in Columbia the river sits beside the borough. If your home is near either, ask your agent specifically about flood and water backup coverage.

Questions Silver Spring homeowners ask

Does insurance pay to remove mold after a burst pipe?

It often can when mold follows a covered water loss, but limits apply. Read your policy and ask your agent.

Will insurance cover mold from a leak I did not notice?

Often not. Policies commonly exclude long-term seepage. The date you first noticed the problem and the cause will matter.

Should I fix the leak before filing a claim?

Stop the leak to prevent more damage, but document it first. Tell your insurer what you did.
